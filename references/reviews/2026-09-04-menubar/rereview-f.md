## Findings

### 1. **Material** — `apps/studio/src/app/studio-toolbar.tsx:42`: the ring it adds is inset, so it paints *over* the pressed chip's ink border — while a chip has keyboard focus, its pressed state is signalled by a 1.19:1 fill and the label colour, the exact failure this wave spent three commits closing

`LANDMARK_FOCUS` (`apps/studio/src/app/studio-regions.ts:22-23`) ends in `focus-visible:-outline-offset-2`. A negative offset moves the outline edge *inside* the border box, and the outline is drawn outward from there — so a 2px ring at −2px offset covers the chip's whole 1px border plus 1px of fill. Measured live at 1512×982 @2×, the focused pressed "Top shelf" chip computes `outline: solid 2px oklch(0.64 0.17 45) offset -2px` over `border-top: 1px solid oklch(0.24 0.014 95)` — and the ink border is not visible in the render. `/tmp/focused-pressed.png` and `/tmp/focused-unpressed.png` (device-scale crops of the chip row, Navigation collapsed) show it directly: the focused chip is an orange rounded rect in both states, while its three unfocused neighbours keep their ink outlines.

What is left to separate the two focused states is the fill and the label:

| channel | light | dark |
|---|---|---|
| pressed fill vs shelf paint (`oklch(0.93 0.008 95)` on `oklch(0.99 0.004 95)`) | **1.19:1** | **1.39:1** |
| label `text-ink` vs `text-ink-muted` | colour only | colour only |

1.19:1 is the same number `tools/src/verify/menubar.mjs:178` prints for the *unpressed* chip's border when it wants that assertion to be red, and the check's own title at `:176` is "state is not signalled by ink alone". Under focus, it is.

Two more consequences of the same offset. The ring's inner neighbour becomes the pressed fill rather than the shelf, at **2.93:1** light — under the 3:1 of WCAG 1.4.11 that `menubar.mjs:177` enforces two lines away (dark is fine at 4.65:1; the outer neighbour is the shelf at 3.29:1 / 7.30:1). And `references/reviews/2026-09-04-menubar/disposition.md:186` records this fix as "the ember ring on focus, the ink outline when pressed", which the render contradicts — they do not coexist.

Nothing catches this: `pressedState` (`menubar.mjs:172-180`) never focuses a chip, and `cockpit.mjs:53` only probes `NAV_SEP` (`:363`).

**Fix I would make:** give the chips a positive offset instead of the landmarks' inset — a sibling constant in `studio-regions.ts`, identical but `focus-visible:outline-offset-2`. I injected exactly that against the live page: the ink border survives underneath, both of the ring's neighbours become the shelf at **3.29:1 light / 7.30:1 dark**, and there is no collision — the toolbar gap is 8px and the ring consumes 4px per side, the last chip ends at 1496 so +4px stays inside 1512. `/tmp/fixed-light.png` is the result.

### 2. **Minor** — `apps/studio/src/app/studio-regions.ts:17-23`: the constant's contract now describes something it is no longer only used for, which is what led to finding 1

The docblock says "A region's landmark can take focus when the control that was pressed unmounts…", "Programmatic focus only: -1 keeps it out of the Tab order", and "inset so the panel's edge does not clip it". All three were true of its four prior call sites — `app.tsx:35`, `studio-toolbar.tsx:24`, `studio-rails.tsx:30` and `:42`, each a `<section tabIndex={-1}>` filling its panel. `studio-toolbar.tsx:42` is the first real Tab stop and the first element that does *not* fill a clipping panel, so the inset that was load-bearing there is the defect here. A reader of the constant has no way to know the offset is panel-specific.

**Fix I would make:** whichever constant the chips end up with, say in the comment why the offset differs — inset for a landmark flush with its panel edge, outset for a control with its own border to preserve.
