## Findings

1. **Material — the `peek` variant's hover zone is rendered inside a panel the same fix makes `inert` and `visibility: hidden`, so `peek` ships with no way back and `peek-all` photographs nothing.** B5/B7's fix settled the shell union by mapping `peek → 'hidden'` (spec:142, spec:200-201), and spec:56-58 states that when `collapse` is `hidden` and the panel is collapsed, `Cockpit.Panel` stamps `inert` and `data-collapsed` on the outer element and the shell's stylesheet sets `visibility: hidden` from it. But spec:140 has `PanelEdge`'s `peek` case render the zone (`data-pc="zone"`), and `PanelEdge` is what the studio's preset swaps into the *collapsed panel's children* (spec:53-54, spec:288) — today's shape at `studio-cockpit.tsx:115` and `:146`. The zone therefore lives inside a zero-width, `inert`, `visibility: hidden` subtree; `inert` suppresses hit-testing and `visibility: hidden` removes it from painting, so the pointer can never reach it. That kills the entire variant (spec:39's "invisible hover zone at the edge of the cockpit body" has no mount point), makes the `['dwell', '[data-pc="zone"]', …]` step at spec:371 a no-op, and turns the `peek-all` baseline into a picture of an empty cockpit. `PanelPeek` is portalled (spec:150) but the zone never is. Either the zone portals to the body like the overlay, or `peek` needs its own shell behaviour that is not `hidden`.

2. **Material — the blocked-hint harness scenario B2 added cannot reach the blocked state at either of the two window sizes it names; both windows are large enough for the expand to act.** Spec:336-339 specifies "at 700px wide ⌃⌘B on a collapsed navigation leaves it collapsed" and "the same at 500px tall for the context shelf". The body group's floor is `navMin` 160 + `centerMinWidth` 320 + `inspectorMin` 200 (`packages/tokens/src/lib/sizes.ts:18,20,27`) = 680px, plus two 1px separators (`--spacing-separator: var(--cs-line)`, `tokens.css:131`, `--cs-p-line: 1px` at `:39`) = 682px; at a 700px viewport the group has 18px of slack and the nav expands, so the assertion is red against a correct implementation. The repo's own calibrated figure disagrees with the spec's: `cockpit.mjs:275` uses **560px** for "a window too narrow to reopen the nav" and `:283-288` asserts exactly that failure there, while 600px (`:248`) is already wide enough that only a *collateral* squeeze rails the inspector. The height case is worse — the centre group's floor is `mainMinHeight` 240 + `contextMin` 120 (`sizes.ts:22,25`) + one separator = 361px, under a 48px pinned top shelf (`topHeight`, `studio-cockpit.tsx:97`) and its separator, so 410px total; at 500px tall there are ~90px spare and the context shelf expands every time. B2's other repairs (per-axis hint text, the clear conditions, the two-argument `['resize', width, height]` step at spec:372) are sound; the numbers the scenario runs at are not.

3. **Material — deferring the focus handoff to the end of the slide opens a 200ms window in which the user can place focus somewhere else, and the handoff then takes it back.** A4's fix is correctly placed — spec:217-219 has `useFocusHandoff` return while `sliding` *before* it reads and clears `pending`, which is exactly the placement `studio-cockpit.tsx:199-200` requires — but the deferral itself creates an interaction today's code cannot have. `noting()` captures `pending.current` at call time (`studio-cockpit.tsx:186-193`) and the effect never re-checks where focus actually is. So: click the toolbar's "Top shelf" button (focus inside `#top`, `pending` true), then click into the manuscript within the 200ms slide; when `sliding` goes false the effect fires, finds `pending` still true, and calls `control.focus()` on the strip's first control (`:203-205`), yanking the caret out of the text the writer just clicked into. Today the effect runs in the same commit as the click, so the window does not exist. Finding 4's disarm makes it fire on that very click rather than 200ms later, which makes it more visible, not less. The cheap repair is one condition at slide end: act only if focus is still inside the region or on `document.body`.

4. **Material — scoping the disarm to "any capture-phase `pointerdown` inside the group element" is right for the body and centre groups but wrong for the root group, whose element contains the entire cockpit including the manuscript.** A1's fix (spec:248-252) does work mechanically: the library registers its own drag detection on `e.element.ownerDocument` at capture (`dist:1499`, `dist:1518`), not on the group, so a group-scoped capture listener does not race it, and every separator drag of that group does start from a `pointerdown` inside the group element — including the ones whose target is a panel because of the inflated 10px grab area the spec cites. But `Cockpit.Panel#top` and `#body` listen on the *root* group's element (`studio-cockpit.tsx:96-150`), which is an ancestor of `#center → #main`. So ⌃⌘T followed by a click anywhere in the manuscript within 200ms cancels the shelf's `flex-grow` transition and snaps it to its end state, and the spec's own justification — "a pointer down anywhere in the group during a 200ms slide costs nothing to honour" (spec:251-252) — is false for the group where the writer's pointer spends all its time. The end state stays correct, so this is motion quality rather than a correctness break, and nothing detects it: the mid-slide baselines are nav-only, and the drag assertion at spec:354-356 uses the navigation's separator in the body group. Restricting the listener to the group's own separators plus its direct panel children, or exempting the root group, keeps A1's benefit without the reach.

5. **Material — B8's fix landed in the `PanelEdge` section and in neither of the two other places that state the same things, so the spec now contradicts itself on the landmark name and on `glyph`.** Spec:131-135 correctly makes the landmark `aria-label={label}` with the control inside it named `Expand <label>`, quoting `cockpit.mjs:205` — which I verified reads `getByRole('region', { name: 'Navigation' }).getByRole('button', { name: 'Expand navigation' })`. Separately, `PanelEdge`'s prop list at spec:130 is still `variant, side, label, onExpand, children?` with no `glyph`, while spec:137-138 says "`PanelEdge` takes `glyph?` to override it" — the same half-landed edit. Both are one-line repairs, but as written the spec answers the question two different ways in two sections.

6. **Minor — the "Sentences that change" list is now correct for every file it names, and still misses the two doc blocks in `cockpit.tsx` that wave 1's own signature change falsifies.** I checked all eight citations B6 added or kept and every one is exact: `studio-toolbar.tsx:17-20`, `friction-notes.md:123-126` and `:86-87`, `useFocusHandoff`'s block at `studio-cockpit.tsx:171-181`, `cockpit.mjs:7-8` ("Nothing vanishes: sidebars collapse to 48px rails … each a landmark with its way back"), `studio-regions.ts:1` ("in toolbar order"), `studio-menus.tsx:8-9` (`VIEW_ORDER`), `studio-commands.ts:71` (`layoutKeys`), plus `AGENTS.md:38` and `:113`. Same class as the five B6 caught, in the file wave 1 edits most.

7. **Minor — the motion paragraph now names the top shelf's pair as A3 asked, but states the wrong pair for the inspector.** Spec:346-350 reads "for the navigation in the body group that is two, the navigation and the centre …, **the same two for the inspector**, the main and context in the centre group, and the top and body in the root group". Read literally, "the same two" names navigation and centre for the inspector; the actual pair is the inspector and the centre, since the inspector is last in the group and the fast path requires every preceding panel to be collapsible and already collapsed, which `#center` (`studio-cockpit.tsx:118`, `minSize` only) is not. The count is right and the assertion as phrased is self-defining ("exactly the panels whose inline `flex-grow` changed"), so only an implementer hardcoding the pair from this sentence is misled — which is the derivation A3 asked to remove. The root-group pair is correct: `#top` and `#body` are the group's only two panels.

8. **Minor — B9's `compact` fix makes the strip's control fit exactly, with zero pixels of slack, and nothing asserts the arithmetic.** Spec:50-51 makes every strip control `compact` "so the 2px focus ring and its offset fit inside the strip's clip". The numbers: 24px box (spec:172) + 2px offset + 2px ring (spec:175, matching `CONTROL_FOCUS`'s `outline-offset-2` at `studio-regions.ts:30-31` and `--cs-p-focus-ring: 2px`) = exactly 32px, and the strip is exactly 32px (`sizes.ts:14`). A one-line spec tying `--panel-toggle-size-compact + 2 × (--panel-focus + --panel-focus-offset) ≤ --panel-strip-size` would make it decidable.

9. **Minor — `hintAt` is specified against an Ark API this workspace cannot check, and the spec never says how three writers of the tooltip's `open` state compose.** `@ark-ui/react` and `@zag-js/*` have **zero** occurrences in `pnpm-lock.yaml` and nothing under `node_modules`, so the round-3 B2 fix rests on behaviour no round has verified against an installed dependency — the spec books Ark's arrival as an accepted risk (spec:439-440) but not this. The design gap is visible without the source: spec:87-89 has `hintAt` open the tooltip for two seconds without hover, spec:123 has a story set the Tooltip's `open` prop directly, and spec:111-112 has hover and focus open it through Ark's own defaults. In Zag's controlled model, supplying `open` moves the machine's open state out of the machine, and pointer/focus events then only call `onOpenChange`; a naive implementation that drives `open` from `hintAt` loses the "and again on hover" clause at spec:260 the moment the first hint has been shown. One sentence — the kit computes `open` as the union of its internal state and the hint window, and reflects `onOpenChange` back into the internal state — closes it.

## Verdict

Twelve of the sixteen round-3 rows hold as written, and the four I could settle by running something — the `fill` channel in jsdom 27.4.0 and Chromium, the easing and `!important` clip in Chromium, the `--cs-ink` distance in both schemes, the `./cockpit.css` route against the menubar's proven resolution — all came back green against the installed sources rather than against the spec's account of them. What did not survive is one architectural contradiction the union fix introduced (the `peek` zone now lives inside the subtree the same fix makes `inert` and invisible, so the variant has no way back), one harness scenario whose two window sizes are both too large to reach the state they exist to prove, and two consequences of the slide that the spec does not yet acknowledge — a focus handoff that can fire after the user has moved focus elsewhere, and a disarm whose group is the whole cockpit; the remaining five are half-landed edits and knife-edge arithmetic, each a sentence or a number.
